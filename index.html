Projects I’ve worked on

Agromega - Plant diary and planner

I took Agromega from the initial idea to launch. I mapped the product’s business processes, defined what data it collects and how that data is organized and displayed, and designed the product logic and user flows. I also built the front end.

Dashboard

A daily overview with active plants, actions of the week, plants that need attention, seasonal harvest, and a practical daily recommendation.

Diary cards

Growing zones with plant groups, tags, last action, active plant count, action count, and fast actions for watering, harvest, photo, notes, and more.

History timeline

A chronological care log that groups actions by date, supports plant filters, shows disease and watering events, and connects actions with photos and AI advice.

Plant collection

Structured plant profiles with variety, custom name, planting date, plant age, diary links, status, last care action, and active/archive states.

This case demonstrates how I approach an ambiguous product problem: research the context, define the logic and MVP, break the work into clear tasks, guide AI-assisted implementation, test the result, and iterate without losing sight of the user or the product goal.

Luna - Women’s cycle and wellbeing calendar

I’m developing Luna from the initial idea into a mobile-first cycle and wellbeing calendar. I’m shaping the main user scenarios and how cycle information supports everyday planning. The aim is to offer practical guidance without making the experience feel clinical or overwhelming.

Daily rhythm

A Today screen with cycle phase, day navigation, care plan, water goal, movement, fasting rhythm, mood tracker, and gentle reminders.

Nutrition logic

A vegan nutrition flow with meal logging, approximate macro analysis, daily totals, plant variety, and phase-aware food recommendations.

Fasting timer

A flexible night-break timer with 13, 16:8, 18:6, 20:4, 36-hour, and custom plans, including clear safety messaging.

Cycle system

A 28-day calendar, four cycle phases, insights, profile settings, localStorage persistence, and mobile-first interaction patterns.

This case demonstrates product thinking in a sensitive consumer-health context: reducing cognitive load, shaping daily scenarios, prioritizing features, designing clear data presentation, writing careful microcopy, and using AI-assisted development to move from an idea to a testable product.

Custom HubSpot modules and a live CTF system for developers

At Wizer, a cybersecurity education SaaS startup, I owned the HubSpot-based marketing website and turned recurring page requests into a reusable content system. The outcome was not simply a collection of modules: it was a working structure that reduced the team's dependency on developers, made routine updates faster, and gave marketing and content colleagues a clearer way to launch and improve pages.

  • Translated marketing, content, design, and SEO needs into reusable page patterns and practical implementation requirements.
  • Built a custom HubSpot theme and around 30 reusable modules using HubL, HTML, CSS, and basic JavaScript.
  • Supported around 50 landing pages, website pages, templates, and reusable sections while keeping the experience consistent.
  • Designed drag-and-drop editing workflows so content managers could publish and update pages independently.
  • Owned implementation, responsive behavior, SEO requirements, QA, issue resolution, and ongoing iteration across the system.
